# Резерв: задачи для других разделов

Задачи прошли тот же отбор и слепую проверку решаемости, что и задачи из основных списков, но по
теме относятся к другим разделам. Когда дойдём до этих разделов, они войдут в их списки
(`proposals/<Раздел>/…`), а статьи будут положены рядом. Решения здесь не приводятся.

## Polynomials

**1. (IMC level 2/5)** Let $p$ be a real polynomial of degree at most $n$, and let $E\subset[0,1]$ be a union
of finitely many closed intervals of total length $\lambda>0$. Suppose that $|p(x)|\le1$ for all $x\in E$.
Prove that $|p(x)|\le(6/\lambda)^n$ for all $x\in[0,1]$.
*Based on:* Y. Yomdin, *Smooth rigidity and Remez inequalities via topology of level sets*,
[arXiv:2106.06961](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.06961), Theorem 6.1. CC BY 4.0.

**2. (IMC level 3/5)** Let $p$ be a real polynomial of degree $n\ge1$ all of whose roots are real and lie
in $[-1,1]$. Prove that $\max_{[-1,1]}|p'|\ge\frac{\sqrt n}{6}\max_{[-1,1]}|p|$.
*Based on:* M. A. Komarov, *On reverse Markov–Nikol'skii inequalities for polynomials with restricted
zeros*, [arXiv:2405.19158](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.19158), inequality (2) (Turán's inequality). CC BY 4.0.

**3. (IMC level 3/5)** Let $n\ge1$ and let $a_0,\dots,a_n,b_0,\dots,b_n$ be real numbers with
$0\le a_i\le b_i$ for all $i$ and $b_0+\dots+b_n>0$. Put
$r(x)=\dfrac{a_0+a_1x+\dots+a_nx^n}{b_0+b_1x+\dots+b_nx^n}$. Prove that $|x\,r'(x)|\le\frac n4$ for every $x>0$.
*Based on:* M. Stephan, *Sharpness characterizes Hill functions*,
[arXiv:2606.11426](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11426), Theorem 3.1 and Corollary 3.3. CC BY 4.0.

**4. (IMC level 4/5)** Let $f$ be a real polynomial of degree $n\ge1$ whose zeros are all real and lie in
$(-\infty,-1]\cup[1,\infty)$. Suppose that $f$ is monotone on $[-1,1]$. Prove that
$\max_{[-1,1]}|f'|\le\frac n2\max_{[-1,1]}|f|$.
*Based on:* T. Erdélyi, *Inequalities for Lorentz polynomials*,
[arXiv:1406.2843](https://arxiv.org/abs/1406.2843), Theorem 2.5 (a claim of Erdős). CC BY 3.0.

## Probability Theory

**1. (IMC level 4/5)** Prove that there exists a Lebesgue measurable set $S\subset[0,\infty)$ such that
the measure of $S\cap[m,m+1]$ tends to $1$ as $m\to\infty$ (over positive integers $m$), but $S$ contains no
infinite arithmetic progression $\{a,a+d,a+2d,\dots\}$ with $d>0$. (The natural solution is a random
construction.)
*Based on:* L. Bradford, H. Kohut, Y. Mooroogen, *Large subsets of Euclidean space avoiding infinite
arithmetic progressions*, [arXiv:2205.04786](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.04786), Theorem 1.1 and Theorem B.
CC BY 4.0.
