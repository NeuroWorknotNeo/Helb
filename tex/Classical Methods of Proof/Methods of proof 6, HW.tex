\noindent\textbf{\underline{Theoretical minimum.}}\quad A direct proof, a proof of the
contrapositive $\neg B\Rightarrow\neg A$ and a proof by contradiction are three different things;
if the contradiction you reach is exactly the negation of the hypothesis, you have written a
contrapositive proof with a superfluous negation. Negation with quantifiers reverses them:
$\neg\forall x\,\exists y\,P$ is $\exists x\,\forall y\,\neg P$, and most failed arguments start
from a wrong negation. For statements ``no object with property $P$ exists'' contradiction is
honest; for existence statements it is a choice which costs the example (Methods of proof -- 5).
Impossibility is proved by an invariant (Methods of proof -- 4), by parity or a colouring, by
counting or dimension, by descent (Methods of proof -- 2), by the extremal choice (Methods of
proof -- 3), or by mapping the problem into a smaller structure~--- residues, determinant, degree,
sign, valuation. For irrationality the standard scheme is to assume $\alpha=p/q$ and produce an
integer $N$ with $0<|N|<1$; a constructible number has degree a power of two over $\mathbb Q$,
which is why the cube cannot be doubled and the angle cannot be trisected.

\noindent\rule{\textwidth}{0.4pt}

\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=0.4em,topsep=7.4pt]
\item Show that there is no arithmetic progression with three terms inside the geometric sequence
$\{2^k\}_{k\ge0}$.\src{VJIMC}

\item Consider unit cubes in $\mathbb R^n$ with edges parallel to the axes and pairwise disjoint
interiors. Prove that among any $2^n+1$ such cubes some two are disjoint~--- or, in the equivalent
form, prove that $2^n+1$ such cubes cannot all pairwise touch; show that $2^n$ pairwise touching
cubes exist.\src{\foreignlanguage{russian}{МФТИ}}

\pagebreak
\item Show that there does not exist a function $f\colon\mathbb R\to\mathbb R$ with the following
property: for every convergent sequence $(a_i)$ of pairwise distinct real numbers the sequence
$(f(a_i))$ converges to no real number.\src{IMS 2022}

\item Prove that a plane figure which has been cut into $n$ squares with sides
$1,\frac{11}{10},\dots,\bigl(\frac{11}{10}\bigr)^{n-1}$ parallel to the axes cannot be cut into
fewer than $n$ rectangles.\src{\foreignlanguage{russian}{МФТИ}}

\item Let $a,b,c$ be integers such that $b^2-4ac$ is not a perfect square. Prove that for every
$n>1$ there are $n$ consecutive natural numbers none of which can be written as
$(ax^2+bxy+cy^2)^z$ with integers $x,y$ and an integer $z>0$.\src{Proposed problems}

\item Prove that a parallelepiped with a base in the plane $Oxy$ cannot be cut into tetrahedra
each of which has a face parallel to $Oxy$.\src{\foreignlanguage{russian}{МФТИ}}

\item Do there exist two commuting linear operators on $\mathbb R^3$ such that no basis puts both
of their matrices into Jordan form?\src{\foreignlanguage{russian}{Мехмат 2006}}

\item Prove that no continuous injection $[0,1]^2\to\mathbb R$ exists, and that no continuous
bijection $[0,1]\to[0,1]^2$ exists, although a continuous surjection does (Topology of the real
line -- 3). Identify in each case what exactly is the invariant that makes the object impossible.

\item Prove that the numbers $\sqrt[3]2$ and $\cos20^\circ$ have degree $3$ over $\mathbb Q$, and
deduce that the cube cannot be doubled and the angle of $60^\circ$ cannot be trisected by ruler
and compass. State precisely the theorem about degrees of constructible numbers that you are
using.

\item Find the error in the following argument and classify it: ``Suppose $\sum1/n$ converges to
$S$. Grouping the terms in pairs gives $\sum(1/(2k-1)+1/(2k))>\sum1/k=S$, hence $S>S$,
a contradiction; therefore the harmonic series diverges.'' Then give a correct proof by
contradiction of the same statement, and a correct direct proof, and say which one you would
submit.
\end{enumerate}
