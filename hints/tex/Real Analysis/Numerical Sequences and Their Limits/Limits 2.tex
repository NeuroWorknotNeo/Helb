\hintstitle{Limits -- 2. Recurrences solvable in closed form}{Class work}

\problem{1}
\hint{1} By induction $x_n>0$. Compute $x_n-\sqrt c$ and $x_n+\sqrt c$ separately: $x_n\pm\sqrt c=\dfrac{(x_{n-1}\pm\sqrt c)^2}{2x_{n-1}}$. Hence the ratio $q_n=\dfrac{x_n-\sqrt c}{x_n+\sqrt c}$ satisfies $q_n=q_{n-1}^2$. (This is the substitution $x_n=\sqrt c\coth\theta_n$, which turns the recurrence into $\theta_n=2\theta_{n-1}$.)
\hint{2} So $q_n=q_0^{2^n}$, where $|q_0|<1$ because $x_0>0$, and $x_n=\sqrt c\,\dfrac{1+q_n}{1-q_n}\to\sqrt c$. Moreover $x_n-\sqrt c=\dfrac{2\sqrt c\,q_0^{2^n}}{1-q_0^{2^n}}$: for $n\ge1$ the error is nonnegative and is essentially squared at each step (quadratic convergence), so the number of correct digits roughly doubles each step.
\hint{3} Newton's method for $g(x)=x^2-c$ is $x_n=x_{n-1}-g(x_{n-1})/g'(x_{n-1})$; check that this is exactly the given recurrence.

\problem{2}
\hint{1} Following the suggestion, express $a_n=(b_n^2-1)/24$ and compute $b_{n+1}^2=1+24a_{n+1}$ in terms of $b_n$. It turns out to be a perfect square.
\hint{2} One gets $b_{n+1}^2=\bigl(\frac{b_n+3}{2}\bigr)^2$, so $b_{n+1}=\frac{b_n+3}{2}$ with $b_1=5$, a linear recurrence: $b_n-3=2^{2-n}$.
\hint{3} Hence $a_n=\dfrac{(3+2^{2-n})^2-1}{24}=\dfrac13+\dfrac1{2^n}+\dfrac{2}{3\cdot4^n}=\dfrac{(2^n+1)(2^n+2)}{3\cdot 4^n}$.

\problem{3}
\hint{1} Hyperbolic substitution in algebraic form: write $a_n=\dfrac{u_n+u_n^{-1}}{\sqrt5}$ with $u_n\ge1$ (i.e.\ $a_n=\frac2{\sqrt5}\cosh\theta_n$). Then $5a_n^2-4=(u_n-u_n^{-1})^2$, so the root equals $u_n-u_n^{-1}$.
\hint{2} With $\tau=\frac{1+\sqrt5}2$ one has $\frac{3\pm\sqrt5}2=\tau^{\pm2}$, and the recurrence becomes $a_{n+1}=\dfrac{\tau^2u_n+\tau^{-2}u_n^{-1}}{\sqrt5}$, i.e.\ $u_{n+1}=\tau^2u_n$. Since $\tau+\tau^{-1}=\sqrt5$, $u_0=\tau$ and $u_n=\tau^{2n+1}$; by Binet's formula (odd index) $a_n=\dfrac{\tau^{2n+1}+\tau^{-(2n+1)}}{\sqrt5}=F_{2n+1}$, the Fibonacci numbers.
\hint{3} Compute $b_n=0,1,1,4,9,25,\dots$ and prove $b_n=F_n^2$ by induction: $b_{n+1}=a_n-b_n=F_{2n+1}-F_n^2=F_{n+1}^2$ by the identity $F_n^2+F_{n+1}^2=F_{2n+1}$. Finally $b_{2n+1}=F_{2n+1}^2=a_n^2$.

\problem{4}
\hint{1} Theorem 1: the characteristic roots of $\lambda^2-6\lambda+8$ are $2$ and $4$; the right-hand side $n=n\cdot1^n$ and $1$ is not a root, so look for a particular solution $An+B$.
\hint{2} Substituting gives $A=\frac13$, $B=\frac{10}{9}$. Then fit $a_n=C2^n+D4^n+\frac n3+\frac{10}9$ to $a_0=1$, $a_1=2$.
\hint{3} $C=-\frac12$, $D=\frac7{18}$, i.e.\ $a_n=\dfrac{7\cdot4^n-9\cdot2^n+6n+20}{18}$ (check: $a_2=6$).

\problem{5}
\hint{1} Write $\alpha=\cos\theta$ with $\theta\in[0,\pi]$ and show by induction that $a_n=\cos n\theta$, using $\cos(n+2)\theta+\cos n\theta=2\cos\theta\cos(n+1)\theta$. Then $|a_n|\le1$ is immediate.
